# Florida Window Tint Law

Governed by **Fla. Stat. §§ 316.2951–316.2956** ("sunscreening material"),
enforced by FLHSMV.

> **Verify before go-live.** These limits have been stable for years, but Jose
> should confirm them against the current FLHSMV statute text before the bot
> starts stating them to customers. A wrong number here means a customer with a
> ticket. Re-check annually.

## VLT limits — how much light must get through

Florida law is written as a **minimum light transmission**. "More than 28%"
means at least 28% of light must pass through. Lower number = darker.

Florida splits vehicles into two classes, and they are treated very differently
in the back:

### Passenger cars (sedans, coupes)

| Window | Legal limit |
|---|---|
| Windshield | Non-reflective tint allowed **above the AS-1 line** only |
| Front side windows | More than **28%** VLT |
| Back side windows | More than **15%** VLT |
| Rear window | More than **15%** VLT |

### SUVs, vans, and trucks (multipurpose passenger vehicles)

| Window | Legal limit |
|---|---|
| Windshield | Non-reflective tint allowed **above the AS-1 line** only |
| Front side windows | More than **28%** VLT |
| Back side windows | More than **6%** VLT |
| Rear window | More than **6%** VLT |

**The practical takeaway:** front windows are the same for everybody — 28%. The
back is where SUVs and vans get to go much darker (6%) than sedans (15%).

## The AS-1 line

A mark etched on most windshields, usually a few inches down from the top. Tint
is allowed **above** it. If a windshield has no AS-1 marking, the standard
practice is to treat the top 5 inches as the allowed band.

## Reflectivity

Separate from darkness — this is about mirrored or metallic finish:

- Front side windows: no more than **25%** reflective
- Back side windows: no more than **35%** reflective

## Other requirements

- **Dual side mirrors** are required if the rear window is tinted.
- **Compliance sticker** — Florida requires a label between the film and the
  glass identifying the tint as legal. The installer affixes it. This is on us,
  not the customer.
- **Medical exemption** — Florida allows an exemption for certain medical
  conditions (§ 316.29545) with physician certification, issued through FLHSMV.
  The customer applies; we don't grant it. If someone raises this, **hand off to
  Jose** — never advise on qualifying for one.

## ⚠️ The trap: film VLT is not net VLT

**The law measures light through the finished window — film plus glass together
— not the film's rating on the box.**

Factory glass already blocks some light. Typical factory auto glass sits around
70–80% VLT on its own. So a 35% film on 75% glass nets out around 26% — under
the 28% front-side limit, even though the film says 35%.

This matters even more on SUVs: many come with **factory privacy glass** on the
back windows, already down around 20% VLT before any film goes on. Adding film
on top of that can drop straight past the 6% floor.

**Bot rule:** never tell a customer a specific film percentage will be legal on
their car. The film number and the finished number are different, and the gap
depends on their glass. Jose measures and picks the film that lands legal.

## What the bot may and may not say

**May say:**
- The legal limits in the tables above, as Florida law
- That front sides are 28% for every vehicle
- That SUVs and vans can go much darker in back than sedans
- That we always install to a legal configuration
- That factory glass affects the finished number, so the film rating alone
  doesn't determine legality

**Must not say:**
- That a specific film choice will be legal on their specific vehicle — Jose
  confirms that by measuring
- Anything about beating, fighting, or getting out of a tint ticket
- Whether someone qualifies for a medical exemption
- Advice on how dark they can go "without getting pulled over" — we quote the
  law, not enforcement odds

**Hand off to Jose** for: existing tickets, medical exemptions, out-of-state
vehicles or recent transplants asking whether their current tint is legal here,
and anyone pushing to go darker than legal.
